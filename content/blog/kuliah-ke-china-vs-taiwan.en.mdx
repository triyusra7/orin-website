"Should I study in China or Taiwan?" It's the question we hear most often in consultations. The honest answer: both are excellent — they just suit different profiles. Let's break it down.

## Cost: both are budget-friendly, with caveats

The good news first — tuition in both China and Taiwan is far more affordable than in most Western countries.

- **China:** undergraduate tuition typically runs RMB 20,000–35,000 per year (roughly USD 2,800–4,900). Living costs in Beijing or Shanghai are higher, but tier-2 cities like Hangzhou or Chengdu are much easier on the wallet.
- **Taiwan:** tuition is around NT$100,000–120,000 per year (roughly USD 3,200–3,800), and living in Taipei costs about USD 450–650 per month. Students can also work part-time up to 20 hours a week with a work permit.

With a scholarship, those numbers can drop dramatically — sometimes to zero.

If you've been learning simplified characters, switching to traditional takes some adjustment — very doable, but budget the time. Universities in both places also offer English-taught programs, so you can start your degree while still levelling up your Mandarin.

## Scholarships: CSC vs MOE & Huayu

- **China** offers the **CSC (Chinese Government Scholarship)**, which can cover tuition, dormitory, and a monthly stipend. Provincial-government and university scholarships add even more routes.
- **Taiwan** offers the **MOE Taiwan Scholarship** for degree study and the **Huayu Enrichment Scholarship (HES)** for 3–12 months of language study.

CSC is competitive but the quota is large. Taiwan's quotas are smaller, yet fewer people apply — with a well-prepared file, your odds are genuinely good.

## Culture and daily life

China offers scale: enormous campuses, vast industry networks, and life inside the world's second-largest economy. Taiwan offers a more relaxed pace, famously friendly locals, and a growing Muslim community with halal food increasingly easy to find in major cities. On internet access, Taiwan is unrestricted; in China you'll adapt to the local app ecosystem.

## Careers after graduation

Graduates of top Chinese universities (Tsinghua, Peking, Zhejiang) are highly sought after by companies doing business with China. Taiwan shines in semiconductors and tech — plus relatively open policies for international graduates who want to stay and work.

## So, which one?

- Choose **China** if you want big-name universities, China-facing business networks, and large scholarship quotas.
- Choose **Taiwan** if you prefer a gentler pace of life, smaller classes, and a tech-focused career path.
