YCT, HSK, TOCFL — three acronyms that confuse plenty of parents and students. The concept is actually simple: all three are standardised Mandarin proficiency tests; they just target different audiences and come from different places. Let's untangle them.

## What are YCT, HSK, and TOCFL?

- **YCT (Youth Chinese Test):** China's official test for **children and teens** (generally under 15). The material is light and child-friendly — a perfect warm-up ladder before HSK.
- **HSK (Hanyu Shuiping Kaoshi):** the official Standard Chinese test for **teens and adults**. This is the certificate universities and scholarship providers in China ask for.
- **TOCFL (Test of Chinese as a Foreign Language):** **Taiwan's** official test, written in traditional characters. Required by Taiwanese universities and government scholarships.

One crucial note: HSK 3 is **not** the same as TOCFL 3. The scales differ — TOCFL Level 3 sits roughly at HSK 4–5. Aim at the right target!

## Which level do universities require?

- **Mandarin-taught bachelor's in China:** usually **HSK 4**; top universities like Tsinghua or Fudan often require **HSK 5** with a minimum score.
- **Bachelor's in Taiwan:** usually **TOCFL Level 3 (B1)**; some programs accept Level 2.
- **English-taught programs:** typically no Mandarin requirement, but an HSK/TOCFL certificate still strengthens a scholarship file.
- **Scholarships:** CSC generally follows the university's requirement; Taiwan's MOE scholarship requires TOCFL for Mandarin-taught programs.

## How long does preparation take?

With consistent study — two to three sessions a week plus self-practice:

- **HSK 1–2:** about 3–4 months per level — pronunciation foundations and ~300 words.
- **HSK 3:** another 4–6 months — you start reading full sentences comfortably.
- **HSK 4:** another 6–8 months — this is the main gateway for university admission.
- **HSK 5:** another 8–12 months — the level where studying entirely in Mandarin feels realistic.
- **TOCFL Level 3:** similar timing to HSK 4, plus 1–2 months to adjust to traditional characters if you learned simplified.

Kids who climb the YCT ladder first usually move much faster when they switch to HSK — their ear for tones is already built.

## The strategy we recommend

1. Decide your **destination country** first — that determines HSK vs TOCFL.
